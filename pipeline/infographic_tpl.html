What the IT community actually uses for AI work

I wanted to know what people actually run, not what gets announced. So I went through comments from Hacker News, Lobste.rs and Tildes posted in 2026, plus a pile of personal blogs. Each person counts once per tool, and only if they say they use it now. This edition also looks at the interface people work in and at how the three communities differ.

Methodology and limitations

Process

  1. I picked 21 Ask HN threads about setups and workflows by hand (19 had 2026 comments) and 213 Lobste.rs and Tildes discussions about using AI in practice.
  2. I downloaded the full comment trees and kept only comments from 2026 ().
  3. An LLM read every one of them, no keyword pre-filter, and pulled out the tool, whether the person still uses it, the interface, the host and which model runs in which harness.
  4. Blogs came from the HN front page and my own Readwise Reader library and feeds (including Swamp Club). I triaged them by title and summary, then an LLM read the full text. Only the author counts.
  5. Tool names were normalised, duplicates removed per author and tool, and the latest statement wins.

Counting rules

  • One person, one tool, one vote per site. The same handle on two sites counts twice, because I can't tell if it is the same person.
  • Only the author's own current use counts, not recommendations or "I've heard".
  • Interfaces are shown two ways: what people said, and what I inferred from the tool's default.
  • The surveys are only a reference point. They are not mixed into the rankings.
  • Tools with fewer than 3 users are left out of the charts.

Biases and limitations

  • People who comment on HN, Lobste.rs and Tildes are not typical developers. They lean toward the terminal and open source.
  • One June HN thread about local models pushes up Qwen, DeepSeek, Pi and OpenCode.
  • Swamp is the product behind the Swamp Club feed, so I left it out of the comparison between sources.
  • No Reddit. Its Responsible Builder Policy requires approval for API access and does not allow research outside the Reddit for Researchers program.
  • The LLM can misread things. As a check, 99.6% of the extracted quotes match the source comment word for word.
